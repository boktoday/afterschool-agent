The Two Programmes

One for the school years. One for after they end.

Both programmes put a personal AI agent in a young person's hands and real projects in front of them. What changes is the rhythm: one runs to the school calendar, in a private online group with a mentor; the other runs to the young person's own clock, with peers in the same kind of group.

While they're at high school

  • A live session after school, once a week, in a small private online group: they build together, with a vetted mentor leading.
  • The agent runs the thread between sessions: the morning minute, the afternoon check-in, tomorrow's plan.
  • They learn by building real things, from a library of forty builds mapped to the eight essentials: personal software, something for the family, something for a local shop.
  • Guardians see how it is going. The work itself stays private, on the young person's own machine.
  • Term by term, at a set time each week: the group meets online. Free places are funded for families who need them.

First after-school groups are forming now

After high school is done

  • For school leavers: no lectures, no timetable. Projects teach, peers review each other's work, and finishing one unlocks the next.
  • Their own pace, around work, study or life: the agent holds the curriculum, and they hold the week.
  • A private online group of up to twenty: people to build with at their own pace, and somewhere to belong.
  • The ladder continues from the school programme: the same agent, the same log, nothing starts over.
  • A low fee where that is fair, waived where it is not, with sponsored places for those who need them.

In Real Life

Between sessions, the world is the classroom.

The group meets online, then the week takes over: a small practice in the morning, a mission on the walk home, a build at the kitchen table. The phone they already carry is part of it, never the point of it.

An assistant. Not a companion.

This is a personal assistant and an agent. It helps a young person be more productive and more creative, and it gives them time back in the real world, so that time can go into life skills and real skills, learned by doing them rather than by talking about them. A personal agent, on their own machine

They install it themselves in about an hour, and shape it over time. A morning minute, an afternoon check-in, a plan for tomorrow. It teaches the eight essentials, one small thing a day, and it never pretends to be more than an assistant.

Why It Matters

Life skills and AI skills. Two of the most important requirements for the future.

There is no better way to learn AI skills in a soft way than while learning the eight essentials. The two run side by side, one small thing a day.

Stay human

The eight keep the human things human: silence without a screen, a body that works, skills in your hands, people who know your name, knowledge that stays when the Wi-Fi drops, time outside, faith that holds under pressure. No agent can do these for you, and this one never pretends to.

Use AI constructively

You install your own agent, shape it, and run it every day. Setting it up teaches you what these systems are. Living with one teaches judgement: when to reach for the machine, when to think alone, and how to keep the ideas your own. It works for you, never instead of you.

No hype. No fear. Just daily practice.

For the Screen-Born Generation

Learning to notice, not just to scroll.

Time outside is one of the eight essentials, so the practice leaves the desk: a photo of the sky, a walk with no destination, a minute of silence before the day starts. The agent teaches the habit. The world does the rest.
